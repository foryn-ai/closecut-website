### When this theme fits

This theme fits when the main problem is not one decision or one event, but how conversations keep breaking down. You may start with a small issue and end up in the same fight. You may avoid topics because it never goes well. You may know the pattern by heart and still not know how to stop it.

It often fits when criticism, defensiveness, shutdown, sarcasm, pursuing, or long silences have become routine. It also fits when one person wants immediate clarity and the other needs less pressure to stay engaged. The intensive is designed for couples who want to practice different skills in the room and leave with a communication plan they can actually use.

### Common pressure patterns

- Escalation loop: A small concern turns into a larger fight within minutes.
- Shutdown and pursue: One person presses for contact while the other withdraws.
- Conflict avoidance: Hard topics get delayed until resentment builds.
- Defensiveness spiral: Each response feels like a correction or counterattack.
- Meaning mismatch: Both partners use the same words but mean different things.
- Repair failure: Even after calm returns, the argument gets reopened with no reset.

### What changes after intensive

After the intensive, the pattern is easier to recognize early. You know what usually starts the cycle, what each person does next, and where to interrupt it. Conversations get more structured because you have rules for pausing, resuming, listening, and making requests. The goal is not perfect agreement. The goal is a more workable way to handle tension without repeating the same damage.

You also leave with a repair routine that helps you come back after a hard moment instead of pretending it did not happen or reopening it without structure. That makes day-to-day communication more direct, more contained, and less exhausting.

### FAQ

**What does communication breakdown mean here?**  
It means your conversations keep getting pulled into the same unhelpful sequence. That might look like escalation, shutdown, avoidance, defensiveness, or repeated misunderstandings around the same topics.

**Can this help if we keep having the same fight?**  
Yes. The intensive maps the trigger, the moves each person makes, and the point where the conversation breaks down. Then you practice a different sequence in session.

**What if one of us shuts down during conflict?**  
Shutdown can be worked with directly. The intensive helps you spot activation earlier, use self-soothing skills, and follow pause and resume rules that reduce pressure.

**What if one of us keeps pushing and the other avoids?**  
That pursue and avoid pattern is common. The intensive focuses on what each move is trying to accomplish, how it backfires, and what to do instead when the pattern starts.

**Will we actually practice communication skills?**  
Yes. This intensive includes live practice with self-soothing, curious listening, clean requests, and short repair moves.

**Can this help if we avoid hard topics altogether?**  
Yes. You build pacing, topic boundaries, and return rules so difficult conversations become more manageable.

**What do we leave with in writing?**  
You leave with a cycle map, pause and resume rules, a repair routine, examples of clean requests, and topic boundaries for the issues that usually trigger conflict.

**Is this about deciding who is right?**  
No. The focus is the pattern between you, not proving a winner. The goal is more accurate, more workable communication.

**What if we have tried therapy before?**  
That can still fit. Many couples understand their pattern in theory but still need structured practice and a concrete home plan.

**How do we start?**
